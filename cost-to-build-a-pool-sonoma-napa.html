How Much Does It Cost to Build a Pool in Sonoma & Napa County?

Fiberglass, gunite, or vinyl — what each actually costs installed in wine country in 2026, including the site conditions and code requirements that most quotes leave out.

By Philip Howard, Owner & General Contractor · CA Lic. #836369 Published August 13, 2026 9 min read
Get a Free Estimate → ☎ (707) 578-6565

Last updated August 13, 2026. Figures reflect real project pricing across Sonoma, Napa, Marin and Solano counties and are reviewed each season.

Key Takeaways

  • A fiberglass pool installed in Sonoma or Napa County runs $65,000–$110,000; a custom gunite pool runs $95,000–$220,000+ depending on size, shape, and finish.
  • The pool shell is usually only 55–65% of the total. Decking, fencing, equipment, gas, and electrical make up the rest.
  • California requires a code-compliant safety barrier plus at least one additional drowning-prevention feature — budget $6,000–$20,000 for fencing and covers.
  • Hillside lots, poor soil, and difficult equipment access are the most common cost multipliers, easily adding $15,000–$50,000.
  • Realistic timeline is 8–16 weeks of construction after permits, which themselves take 3–10 weeks in most local jurisdictions.

Gunite dominates in Napa and Sonoma for one practical reason: our lots slope. A gunite shell can be engineered into a grade, cantilevered, or built with a vanishing edge that reads against a vineyard or valley view. Fiberglass shells are manufactured off-site in fixed forms and need a relatively level excavation and clear crane access.

A note on quoted prices. A $65,000 pool advertisement almost always refers to the shell and basic equipment on an easy flat site. By the time you add decking, a required safety barrier, gas line, electrical subpanel, and landscape repair, the finished number is commonly 45–70% higher. Ask any bidder for a written scope that explicitly lists what is excluded.

The extras people forget to budget

  • Landscape restoration. Heavy equipment does not tiptoe. Repairing lawn, irrigation, and hardscape afterward commonly runs $5,000–$20,000.
  • Gas line upsizing. A pool heater is a large load. If your existing meter and line cannot carry it, upgrading can cost $2,500–$8,000.
  • Electrical panel capacity. Pumps, heaters, and lighting frequently require a subpanel or a service upgrade, $2,000–$9,000.
  • Water. Filling a 20,000-gallon pool is not free, and during drought restrictions some districts limit or surcharge it.

How do site conditions change the price?

Two identical pools on two different lots can differ by $50,000. The drivers:

  • Slope. A pool on grade is straightforward. A pool set into a hillside needs engineered retaining walls and often deepened footings, adding $15,000–$60,000.
  • Access. Excavators and gunite rigs need a path. If the only route is a 4-foot side yard, material moves by hand or conveyor and labor costs climb 20–40%.
  • Soil. Expansive clay is common across the North Bay and may require deeper footings or a structural slab. Rock requires hammering, which is slow and loud and billed by the hour.
  • Groundwater. High water table areas near the Napa River or Laguna de Santa Rosa may need a hydrostatic relief valve and dewatering during construction.
  • Seismic design. All pool shells here are engineered for seismic loads, which is part of why gunite specification and steel schedules matter more than the sales brochure suggests.

What permits and safety requirements apply in California?

Every in-ground pool in California requires a building permit, and virtually every jurisdiction requires electrical and plumbing permits alongside it. Expect $3,500–$9,000 in permits and engineering, and 3–10 weeks for review.

The Swimming Pool Safety Act

California law requires a permitted residential pool to have an approved enclosure plus at least one additional drowning-prevention safety feature. Accepted options include:

  • An enclosure fence at least 60 inches high with self-closing, self-latching gates that swing away from the pool
  • Removable mesh fencing meeting ASTM F2286
  • An approved safety pool cover meeting ASTM F1346
  • Exit alarms on doors providing direct access to the pool
  • Self-closing, self-latching devices on those doors, with the release at least 54 inches above the floor

Additionally, all new pools must have anti-entrapment drain covers compliant with the Virginia Graeme Baker Act, and pool pumps must meet California Title 20 energy efficiency standards — which in practice means a variable-speed pump.

Do not let anyone build you a pool without pulling permits. An unpermitted pool surfaces at resale, can void homeowner's insurance in the event of an accident, and creates real liability exposure. Correcting it later often means partial demolition to expose work for inspection.

Two decisions cut this meaningfully: a variable-speed pump (now required anyway) and a cover. A quality cover reduces both heating cost and evaporation substantially, and in a region with periodic water restrictions, evaporation is not a trivial concern.

Resurfacing is the major long-cycle expense. Plaster typically needs redoing at 8–12 years; pebble and quartz finishes stretch to 15–20. Budget $9,000–$22,000 when that comes due.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a pool cost in Sonoma County in 2026?

A fiberglass pool installed in Sonoma County costs $65,000 to $110,000 in 2026, and a custom gunite pool costs $95,000 to $220,000 or more. The finished number depends heavily on decking, fencing, and site access. Most complete backyard pool projects in the county land between $120,000 and $180,000.

Is a fiberglass or gunite pool better in Northern California?

Gunite is generally the better choice on the sloped, view-oriented lots common in Sonoma and Napa County, because the shell can be engineered to any shape, depth, or grade and supports vanishing edges. Fiberglass is faster to install, easier to maintain, and less expensive, but requires relatively level ground, crane access, and a standard shape.

Does a swimming pool add value to a home in wine country?

In Sonoma and Napa County, a well-built pool on a property with outdoor living space typically recovers 40 to 60 percent of its cost at resale, higher than the national average because outdoor entertaining is central to the regional lifestyle. A poorly integrated pool that consumes the entire usable yard can reduce value.

How long does it take to build a pool?

Construction runs 8 to 16 weeks after permits are issued, and permitting itself takes 3 to 10 weeks depending on the jurisdiction. Gunite pools require a curing period before plaster, which cannot be compressed. Total realistic time from contract signing to swimming is four to seven months.

Do I need a fence around my pool in California?

Yes. California's Swimming Pool Safety Act requires an approved enclosure for permitted residential pools, plus at least one additional drowning-prevention feature such as a safety cover, door alarms, or self-closing self-latching door hardware. Your building department verifies compliance before issuing a final inspection.
