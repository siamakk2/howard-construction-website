What “we build the whole house” actually means

Plenty of contractors describe themselves as home builders and then subcontract the coordination to someone else, or hand you off to a project manager running fifteen jobs. Here is the complete scope we carry ourselves on a ground-up build:

Ground & Structure

  • Site evaluation, survey coordination, soils report
  • Grading, drainage and retaining
  • Utility trenching, well and septic
  • Engineered slab, stem wall or drilled-pier foundations
  • Framing, structural steel where specified

Envelope

  • Roof structure and assembly, Class A in fire zones
  • Windows and exterior doors
  • Weather-resistive barrier, flashing and siding
  • Insulation and air sealing to Title 24
  • Decks, porches and exterior stairs

Systems

  • Plumbing, gas and water heating
  • Electrical service, panel and full rough-in
  • HVAC, ducting and ventilation
  • Solar and battery storage
  • Residential fire sprinklers, low voltage and networking

Interior

  • Drywall, texture and paint
  • Custom cabinetry and millwork
  • Countertops, tile and stone
  • Flooring and finish carpentry
  • Fixtures, appliances and hardware

Compliance

  • Permit submittal and plan-check corrections
  • Structural engineering coordination
  • Title 24 energy documentation
  • WUI Chapter 7A fire-hardened assemblies
  • Every inspection through certificate of occupancy

Specialty

  • Post-wildfire rebuilds and insurance scope coordination
  • Hillside and steep-slope construction
  • Manufacturer packages including Lindal post-and-beam
  • Accessory dwelling units built alongside the main house
  • Barns, shops and carriage houses

One contractor, one license, one point of responsibility. When the foundation, the framing and the finish carpentry all sit under the same license, nobody can point at anyone else when something does not line up. That is the practical argument for a single builder carrying the whole job.

Before you buy the land

The single most expensive mistake in custom home building is buying a parcel that costs a fortune to build on. The purchase price is only half the question — the other half is what the land will demand before a foundation can be poured.

Site conditionAdded cost
Flat, utilities at the property line, city sewer$35,000–$70,000
Gentle slope, moderate grading, short driveway$70,000–$140,000
Hillside, engineered retaining, long private drive$140,000–$300,000
Steep hillside, deep piers, well and septic, fire access road$300,000–$600,000+

Call us before you close. A two-hour walk of a parcel can surface slope, drainage, access width, soil, septic and utility issues that move your budget by six figures. We do it at no charge, and we would rather tell you a lot is a bad idea than build you an expensive lesson.

Fire-hardened building and rebuilds

Since 2017 this has stopped being an optional consideration in wine country. Much of Sonoma and Napa County is mapped Wildland-Urban Interface, which triggers California Building Code Chapter 7A: Class A roof assemblies, ember-resistant vents, ignition-resistant siding and soffits, enclosed eaves, tempered glazing and decking restrictions within ten feet of the structure.

We have worked through the Tubbs Fire reconstruction, including Coffey Park and Fountaingrove. Rebuilds carry their own demands: insurance scope and draw schedules, foundation assessment, and rebuilding a 1970s house to 2026 code. And increasingly the fire-hardening work determines something beyond compliance — whether the finished home is insurable at all.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who builds custom homes in Santa Rosa and Sonoma County?

Howard Construction Inc. is a family-owned Class B general contractor, CSLB license #836369, headquartered in Santa Rosa and building custom homes across Sonoma and Napa County since 2000. Owner Philip Howard is personally on every job site. Free written estimates are available at (707) 578-6565.

How much does it cost to build a custom home in Sonoma or Napa County?

Custom home construction runs $400 to $850 per square foot in 2026, excluding land. A 2,200 square foot home typically costs $900,000 to $1.87M in hard construction cost. Site work, permits and impact fees are additional, and homes in Wildland-Urban Interface zones add $25,000 to $70,000 for Chapter 7A fire-hardening requirements.

Does Howard Construction handle the whole build, or just part of it?

The whole build. We take a project from raw land through to occupancy: site evaluation and grading, engineered foundations, framing and weather-tight envelope, plumbing, electrical, HVAC and solar, insulation, drywall, cabinetry, tile, flooring and finish carpentry, plus permits, plan-check corrections and every inspection. One licensed contractor is responsible for all of it.

How long does it take to build a custom home in wine country?

Plan on 14 to 24 months from first drawing to certificate of occupancy. Design and engineering take 3 to 7 months, permit review 2 to 5 months, and construction 10 to 14 months. Roughly half the calendar is spent before any ground is broken, which is the part homeowners most often underestimate.

Can you build on a hillside or unimproved lot?

Yes. Hillside and unserviced parcels are common across Sonoma and Napa County. That work involves engineered or drilled-pier foundations, retaining and drainage, a fire-apparatus-accessible driveway, and often a well and septic system. Site work on a steep parcel can range from $140,000 to $600,000 and should be assessed before you buy the land, not after.

Do you build fire-hardened homes and post-fire rebuilds?

Yes. We have worked on reconstruction following the 2017 Tubbs Fire, including Coffey Park and Fountaingrove. Rebuilds involve insurance scope coordination, foundation assessment, and building to current code, which means fire sprinklers, Title 24 compliance, and full Chapter 7A ignition-resistant construction in WUI-designated areas.

Will the owner actually be on my job site?

Yes. Philip Howard runs every project personally rather than assigning a project manager rotating between jobs. The license on your permit belongs to the person you will see on site, which is the practical reason our project count is deliberately limited.
