Where Craft and History Meet

St. Helena's housing stock reads like a timeline of Napa Valley itself: 19th-century stone and Victorian homes near downtown, farmhouse compounds among the vines, and contemporary hillside estates in the Mayacamas and Howell Mountain foothills. Working here means knowing when to restore, when to replicate, and when to quietly modernize behind original walls — judgment that only comes from decades in North Bay construction.

It also means building for fire. The 2020 Glass Fire burned to the edges of St. Helena and through the hills above it, and every hillside project we touch now is designed around ignition resistance: Class A roofing, ember-resistant vents, tempered glazing, ignition-resistant decking, and defensible-space planning baked into the site work rather than bolted on afterward. This isn't upsell — in the WUI zones around St. Helena, it's simply how responsible construction is done in 2026.

Howard Construction has built in Calistoga and the Upvalley since 2000. We know the inspectors, the soil, the water and septic realities of vineyard parcels, and the subcontractors who actually show up. That's what keeps an Upvalley project on schedule.

Building in St. Helena: What We Know That Saves You Time

Historic fabric downtown, wildland-urban interface in the hills, working vineyards in between — three very different building environments in one small city.

Permits & Jurisdiction

Projects inside city limits go through St. Helena's Planning & Building Department, with added review for properties in or near the historic district. Vineyard and hillside parcels outside city limits are handled by Napa County PBES, often with grading and erosion-control requirements.

Fire-Hardened Construction

For WUI parcels we build to Chapter 7A of the California Building Code as the floor, not the ceiling: Class A roofs, ember-resistant venting, ignition-resistant siding and decking, and site design that supports defensible space under PRC 4291.

Estate & Vineyard Logistics

Rural parcels mean wells, septic, long driveways, PG&E coordination, and harvest-season scheduling around your vineyard operations. We plan construction logistics so the build never interferes with the crop.

St. Helena Homeowner Questions, Answered

Can you remodel a historic home in St. Helena?

Yes. Work on or near historic properties requires extra design sensitivity and sometimes additional review, and we build to preserve street-facing character while upgrading structure, systems, and energy performance behind it. Complete submittals with clear historic-compatibility documentation are what keep these approvals moving.

What does fire-hardened construction actually include?

At minimum for WUI parcels: Class A roof assembly, ember-resistant vents, ignition-resistant exterior cladding and decking, tempered or multi-pane glazing, and enclosed eaves — plus site-level defensible space planning. We price these as standard scope on hillside projects, not add-ons.

Do you build on vineyard properties with wells and septic?

Regularly. Rural St. Helena parcels bring water, septic, access, and utility questions that town lots don't, and we scope all of it up front — including coordination with your vineyard manager so construction traffic never conflicts with harvest.

How far in advance should I plan an Upvalley project?

For substantial work, start conversations 6–12 months before you want ground broken. Design, engineering, and permitting on county hillside parcels take real time, and the best subcontractor crews book out. A free early consultation costs nothing and protects your timeline.
