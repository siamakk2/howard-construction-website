How Much Does a Kitchen Remodel Cost in Sonoma & Napa County?

Real 2026 numbers for a wine country kitchen — what each tier actually buys, where the money concentrates, and the three decisions that move the total more than any finish selection.

By Philip Howard, Owner & General Contractor · CA Lic. #836369 Published August 13, 2026 10 min read
Get a Free Estimate → ☎ (707) 578-6565

Last updated August 13, 2026. Figures reflect real project pricing across Sonoma, Napa, Marin and Solano counties and are reviewed each season.

Key Takeaways

  • Most kitchen remodels in Sonoma and Napa County land between $45,000 and $180,000 in 2026, with the majority of full remodels falling in the $70,000–$120,000 band.
  • Cabinetry is typically 30–40% of the entire budget — the single largest line item by a wide margin.
  • Moving the sink, range, or exterior walls is what separates a $60,000 project from a $130,000 one. Layout changes cost far more than finish upgrades.
  • Expect 8–16 weeks of construction, plus 4–10 weeks of design and permitting before demolition starts.
  • Kitchen remodels recover roughly 60–75% of cost at resale in this region, among the highest of any interior project.

Per square foot, wine country kitchens generally run $400 to $1,100. That range is wide because a kitchen is not priced like a bedroom — the cost is driven by fixtures, cabinetry and mechanical work packed into a small footprint, not by area.

The most useful budgeting question is not "what finishes do I want." It is "am I keeping the sink, range and refrigerator where they are." Answer that first, and your budget range narrows dramatically before you have chosen a single tile.

Cabinet pricing, honestly

  • Stock — $180–$350 per linear foot. Fixed sizes, limited finishes, fast lead times.
  • Semi-custom — $350–$650 per linear foot. Adjustable dimensions and a real finish range. Where most quality remodels land.
  • Full custom — $650–$1,400+ per linear foot. Built to your exact space, any species, any detail.

A typical wine country kitchen runs 25–35 linear feet of cabinetry. Multiply that out and you see why cabinets dominate the budget — and why cabinet selection, more than counters or appliances, sets your total.

Why moving the sink costs so much

Homeowners are routinely surprised that relocating plumbing costs more than upgrading from quartz to marble. Here is the actual chain of work behind moving a sink even six feet:

  • Open the floor or ceiling below to access supply and drain lines
  • Re-route drain with correct slope — drains work on gravity and cannot simply bend around obstacles
  • Re-route hot and cold supply, and often the dishwasher line
  • Add or relocate a vent stack, which frequently means opening a wall to the roof
  • Patch, insulate, and refinish everything opened along the way
  • Additional inspections at rough plumbing stage

What California code requires in a kitchen remodel

A permitted kitchen remodel triggers current code on the work you touch, and that catches many older wine country homes off guard:

  • GFCI protection on all countertop receptacles
  • Two dedicated 20-amp small-appliance circuits serving countertop outlets
  • Dedicated circuits for dishwasher, disposal, microwave and range
  • AFCI protection on most branch circuits
  • Outlet spacing such that no point along the counter is more than 24 inches from a receptacle
  • Mechanical ventilation ducted to the exterior, sized to the range
  • Title 24 energy compliance for lighting and any new windows

On homes built before roughly 1980, opening the walls often reveals aluminum wiring, ungrounded circuits, or an undersized panel. A service upgrade to 200 amps runs $4,000–$9,000 and is one of the most common mid-project additions in this region.

Budget a contingency and mean it. Fifteen percent on a kitchen in a home over 30 years old is realistic. Behind the cabinets you may find dry rot from an old leak, failed subfloor, or a vent that was never legal. None of it is visible when the estimate is written.

Plan on 8 to 16 weeks without a kitchen. Cabinet lead time is the pacing item on nearly every job, which is why the order should be placed the moment the layout is final, not after demolition.

Living through it

Set up a temporary kitchen before demolition: refrigerator, microwave, toaster oven, and a wash station in a laundry sink or bathroom. Seal the doorway with plastic and run an air scrubber if you can. The households that handle a remodel best are the ones that plan the inconvenience rather than absorbing it as it comes.

Where to save, and where not to

Genuinely worth spending on

  • Cabinet boxes and hardware. Plywood boxes and quality soft-close hardware are what make a kitchen feel solid in year ten. This is not visible and not glamorous, and it matters more than almost anything else.
  • Ventilation. An undersized or recirculating hood in a kitchen with a serious range is a permanent daily irritation.
  • Electrical. If the panel is marginal, upgrade it now while the walls are open.

Safe places to economize

  • Keep the layout. The single largest saving available to you.
  • Quartz over exotic stone. Durable, consistent, no sealing, and a fraction of the cost of book-matched marble.
  • Reface rather than replace if the existing boxes are solid and the layout works — $12,000–$25,000 versus $40,000+.
  • Mix appliance tiers. Spend on the range and dishwasher; a mid-tier refrigerator performs nearly identically to a premium one.
  • Standard-height uppers. Ceiling-height cabinetry with a stacked upper row is beautiful and adds real cost.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a kitchen remodel cost in Sonoma County in 2026?

A full kitchen remodel in Sonoma County costs $45,000 to $180,000 in 2026, with most complete remodels landing between $70,000 and $120,000. A cosmetic refresh that keeps the existing layout runs $18,000 to $40,000. Cabinetry typically accounts for 30 to 40 percent of the total.

Is it cheaper to reface cabinets or replace them?

Refacing costs $12,000 to $25,000 compared with $40,000 or more for new cabinetry, so it is substantially cheaper. It only makes sense when the existing cabinet boxes are structurally sound and the layout already works, since refacing changes doors, drawer fronts and visible surfaces but leaves the underlying boxes and configuration in place.

Do I need a permit to remodel a kitchen in California?

You need a permit for any electrical, plumbing, mechanical, or structural work, which covers nearly every full kitchen remodel. Purely cosmetic work such as painting, replacing countertops, or swapping a faucet generally does not require one. Because most remodels move or add circuits, assume a permit is required unless your building department says otherwise.

How long is a kitchen out of use during a remodel?

Expect 8 to 16 weeks from demolition to completion, preceded by 4 to 10 weeks of design, selections and permitting. Cabinet lead times of 6 to 16 weeks are usually the pacing item, so ordering cabinets as soon as the layout is finalized is the most effective way to shorten the overall schedule.

What percentage of a kitchen remodel do I get back at resale?

Kitchen remodels typically recover 60 to 75 percent of their cost at resale in Sonoma and Napa County, higher than the national average because kitchens are heavily weighted in wine country home values. Mid-range remodels usually recover a higher percentage than luxury ones, since very high-end kitchens can exceed neighborhood value ceilings.
